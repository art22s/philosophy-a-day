Anekāntavāda — the many-sidedness of reality

Jain traditions of ancient India. Its precise beginnings are debated; the Tattvārtha Sūtra, composed approximately between the 2nd and 5th centuries CE, is an early systematic source.
When
Its roots are associated with early Jain teachings; systematic philosophical development took shape over the first several centuries CE.
Important people
Mahāvīra (traditionally dated to the 6th–5th century BCE); later Jain philosophers including Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmin.
Origin
Jain traditions of ancient India. Its precise beginnings are debated; the Tattvārtha Sūtra, composed approximately between the 2nd and 5th centuries CE, is an early systematic source.

The idea

Reality has many aspects, so a statement about something may be true from one standpoint without capturing the whole. A pot, for example, persists as a substance while its shape and condition change. Jain thinkers developed methods for qualifying claims according to the perspective and sense in which they are made. This is not simply “every opinion is equally true”: each view can be partial, and some claims remain mistaken.

Why it matters

Anekāntavāda offers a way to take disagreement seriously without assuming that one description exhausts a complex subject. It can encourage intellectual humility in debates about identity, science, or public policy, while still asking us to assess evidence and the limits of each perspective.
